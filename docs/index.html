A map of the Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Structure, verification and historical lineage · 21 November 2026

Formally verified on 20 November 2026 by independent kernels. Not yet understood, peer-reviewed or recognized by the Clay Institute.

Orientation for readers of Levent Alpöge’s formalization (announced 20 Nov 2026, found with Claude). This is a guide to the code’s structure, not a verification and not a review of the mathematics. Status of the proof: formally verified by an independent checker: per Sutherland’s verification report (MIT) (20 Nov 2026), all 175,187 modules compile, #print axioms shows only the three standard axioms, and Lean’s leanchecker plus four independently written checkers (nanoda, con-leche, con-ron, lean4lean) accept the proof; Lean’s default kernel (22 Nov) and paranoid kernel (23 Nov) accept the full export, and a sandboxed rebuild reproduced the published export byte for byte. The last stage of the full Comparator run is still in progress. Michael Rubinstein (Waterloo) reports a further independent check without errors (his page). On 21 Nov 2026 a joint statement of the Lean and Mathlib communities (endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative) certified the statement and proof as correct. Human understanding of the argument is still outstanding; a first AI-written explanatory draft for the zeta function (Rubinstein, v2 of 22 Nov) is unfinished.

Assessment · Raissa Ruchti
If the reported formal verification is accurate and the formal theorem genuinely expresses RH without hidden assumptions, then this is substantially more than an ordinary proof claim. It would constitute a rigorous formal proof.
  • The theorem expresses RH: checked. The headline theorem proves Mathlib’s own RiemannHypothesis, with no redefinition in the project.
  • No hidden assumptions: confirmed. The five headline theorems depend only on propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound (Sutherland’s verification report (MIT)); source scan also clean.
  • Verification accurate: confirmed by four checkers. Compiled with zero errors; leanchecker replayed every module with no rejections; the independent checkers nanoda, con-leche and lean4lean accepted (lean4lean after re-running five timeouts with a higher limit); in its compare mode, con-leche also confirmed that all five headline theorems are justified by the solution. Still running there: Lean’s default and paranoid kernels and an end-to-end Comparator run (its statement comparison and axiom check have already passed). The older con-ron build bundled with Lean 4.36 declined because it lacks support for one feature of the proof; that is not a rejection. Sandbox check now done: a separate sandboxed lake build Main (no network, read-only source) finished with zero errors (21 Nov), and the export made there is byte for byte identical to the published one (sha256 5ea74b91…244e). Of the compiled files, 175,186 of 175,187 match the manifest; one (…/RankPointCounts/LocModel.olean) compiles non-deterministically. Sutherland has since traced this to a harmless difference of 38 bytes, the name of a bound variable in an auto-generated lemma, caused by a known Lean issue fixed in Lean v4.36.0; single-threaded compilation reproduces it identically, and the export is unaffected.

      From Euclid to Alpöge: 2,300 years of primes, one checker

      The formally verified proof is the latest step in a line of results about how prime numbers are distributed. Most earlier steps are already checked by the same Lean kernel, in Mathlib or in community projects.

      1. c. 300 BC · Euclid

        There are infinitely many primes (Elements IX.20). In Mathlib: Nat.exists_infinite_primes. Re-proved below in 24 lines.

      2. 1737 · Euler

        The sum over all n of n−s equals a product over all primes: primes meet analysis. In Mathlib: riemannZeta_eulerProduct, alongside Euler’s ζ(2) = π²/6 as riemannZeta_two (Loeffler–Stoll).

      3. 1837 · Dirichlet

        Infinitely many primes in every admissible arithmetic progression, via L-functions. In Mathlib: Nat.setOf_prime_and_eq_mod_infinite (Loeffler–Stoll).

      4. 1859 · Riemann

        “Ueber die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Grösse”: counting primes through the zeros of ζ; the hypothesis appears in passing: “Hiervon wäre allerdings ein strenger Beweis zu wünschen; ich habe indess die Aufsuchung desselben nach einigen flüchtigen vergeblichen Versuchen vorläufig bei Seite gelassen …” Source: Bernhard Riemann, „Ueber die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Grösse“, Monatsberichte der Königlich Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften zu Berlin, November 1859; title and quotation in the original spelling. Transcription: Clay Mathematics Institute; autograph manuscript: SUB Göttingen, Cod. Ms. B. Riemann 3. In Mathlib: riemannZeta, the functional equation riemannZeta_one_sub, and the statement RiemannHypothesis.

      5. 1896 · Hadamard, de la Vallée Poussin

        The prime number theorem: about x / log x primes up to x. Its key input, ζ(s) ≠ 0 on Re s = 1, is in Mathlib as riemannZeta_ne_zero_of_one_le_re; the theorem itself was formalized by the PrimeNumberTheoremAnd project led by A. Kontorovich and T. Tao.

      6. Aug 2026 · Claude

        More than two thirds of the zeros of ζ proved simple and on the critical line (previous record 41.6 %), with a Lean formalization reviewed by Anthropic mathematicians and outside experts.

      7. 20 Nov 2026 · Alpöge with Claude

        Proof of RH and GRH for Dirichlet L-functions, released as Lean code only; formally verified by several independent checkers the same night (Sutherland, MIT). Not yet understood or peer-reviewed. This page.

      Euclid in Lean, checked on 21 Nov 2026

      The ancient argument (n! + 1 has a prime factor larger than n), written out with comments in German, then the same axiom audit Sutherland ran on the RH proof, with the same result. Output: 'euklid' depends on axioms: [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]. Paste it into live.lean-lang.org to check it yourself.

      import Mathlib

/-- Euklid, Elemente IX.20: Über jeder Zahl n liegt eine Primzahl.
    Also gibt es unendlich viele Primzahlen. -/
theorem euklid (n : ℕ) : ∃ p, n ≤ p ∧ Nat.Prime p := by
  -- Euklids Trick: die Zahl n! + 1 (Produkt aller Zahlen bis n, plus 1)
  -- p sei ihr kleinster Primteiler
  let p := Nat.minFac (Nat.factorial n + 1)
  -- n! + 1 ist nicht 1, also hat sie einen Primteiler
  have hf : Nat.factorial n + 1 ≠ 1 := ne_of_gt (Nat.succ_lt_succ (Nat.factorial_pos n))
  have hp : Nat.Prime p := Nat.minFac_prime hf
  -- zu zeigen bleibt: p ist mindestens n
  refine ⟨p, ?_, hp⟩
  -- Annahme des Gegenteils: p < n
  by_contra h
  push_neg at h
  -- dann teilt p die Zahl n! (p kommt im Produkt 1·2·…·n vor)
  have h1 : p ∣ Nat.factorial n := Nat.dvd_factorial (Nat.minFac_pos _) h.le
  -- p teilt auch n! + 1 (so war p gewählt)
  have h2 : p ∣ Nat.factorial n + 1 := Nat.minFac_dvd _
  -- also teilt p die Differenz, nämlich 1
  have h3 : p ∣ 1 := (Nat.dvd_add_right h1).mp h2
  -- aber eine Primzahl teilt 1 nicht: Widerspruch
  exact hp.one_lt.ne' (Nat.dvd_one.mp h3)

      The seven Millennium Prize Problems, November 2026

      Clay Mathematics Institute, 2000; $1 M each. Officially solved: one. Clay’s own labels as of September 2026: Poincaré solved, Navier–Stokes “active”, the rest unsolved. Claims below are listed as claims.

      Poincaré conjecture

      Solved · Perelman 2002–03, recognized 2010

      The only one Clay has declared solved, after roughly four years of community checking. Perelman declined the prize.

      Riemann hypothesis

      Claimed · formally verified · Alpöge with Claude, 20 Nov 2026

      Lean-only proof of RH and GRH for Dirichlet L-functions. Independently compiled and kernel-checked with only standard axioms (Sutherland, MIT), including Lean’s paranoid kernel (23 Nov); last Comparator stage running; certified correct in a joint statement of the Lean and Mathlib communities (21 Nov). Not yet understood or published. This page. Further replication without errors by Michael Rubinstein (Waterloo). Same day, a separate all-human GRH proof draft by Alessandro Masullo (Stanford) was posted by Ravi Vakil; unrefereed. In an update the same day Vakil reported that several people had found problems in it (identified with AI help); it is not accepted.

      Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness

      Claimed twice · OpenAI (forced), 8 Sep 2026 · Alpöge & Buckmaster (unforced), 20 Nov 2026 · Clay: “active”

      Proof plus Lean formalization that a smooth fluid can blow up in finite time, under smooth forcing (statements C/D of the official problem); OpenAI says it will not claim the prize. Credit dispute with Buckmaster and Alpöge, who posted Lean-verified blow-up results for related fluid equations a day earlier. Mathematicians call it likely correct but so far hard to learn from. New on 20 Nov 2026: Alpöge and Buckmaster, with heavy AI assistance, claim finite-time blow-up for the 3D Navier–Stokes equations without any external force, with a Lean formalization; a companion paper with Coiculescu does the same for the Euler equations. Tao calls the Navier–Stokes paper technically human-readable with a broadly clear strategy, but not yet a professionally written proof. Unrefereed.

      Hodge conjecture

      Open · special case claimed (OpenAI, 30 Sep 2026)

      Paper “The rational Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties”: an important family of cases, not the general conjecture. Under review.

      Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture

      Open

      Strong partial results for small analytic rank; no claimed full proof from a recognized source.

      P versus NP

      Open

      Most researchers expect P ≠ NP; no accepted proof in either direction.

      Yang–Mills existence and mass gap

      Open · existence half claimed (OpenAI, Oct 2026)

      OpenAI’s Lean proof that pure quantum Yang–Mills theory exists (Wightman axioms, as a scaling limit of 4D lattice gauge theory) was judged correct by a physicists’ committee chaired by David Gross (statement of 9 Nov 2026). The mass gap, the second half of the Clay problem, is not proved; human understanding still pending. Not a Clay ruling.

      What everyone is waiting for

      Three stages, in order. A green kernel check settles correctness, not understanding.

      1. done · 20 Nov 20261 · The axiom report

        #print axioms on the five headline theorems from an independent full build: only propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. No hidden assumption, no gap. Source: Sutherland’s verification report (MIT).

      2. nearly done · final run in progress2 · Independent checkers

        The Lean kernel is itself a program. The Omega Institute’s Comparator run re-checks the proof with four independently written checkers (Lean paranoid, nanoda, con-leche, con-ron). If all accept, the formal proof is as secure as formal proofs get. So far: leanchecker, nanoda, con-leche, con-ron (--compare, 21 Nov) and lean4lean accept; Lean’s default kernel (22 Nov, 48 h) and paranoid kernel (23 Nov, 72 h) accept the full export; the standalone Comparator v4.33.0 reports “Your solution is okay!” (23 Nov; build step stubbed, challenge module from Sutherland’s own compile). The full lake comparator v4.36.0 run has passed its paranoid-kernel and nanoda stages; its last stage, Lean’s default kernel (about 48 h), is still running; the older con-leche/con-ron builds bundled with Lean v4.36.0 declined for lack of support of one feature (not a rejection). A sandboxed rebuild reproduced the published export byte for byte; Rubinstein (Waterloo) reports a further error-free check. On 21 Nov the joint statement of the Lean and Mathlib communities certified the proof as correct. Sources: Sutherland’s report, Lean community blog, Rubinstein’s page.

      3. begun · months to years3 · Understanding

        Why does the argument work? Which idea is new, which of the 561 literature statements are load-bearing, and can 17.4 M lines be condensed into something a person can read? Only then would a journal publish it; Clay considers a solution at the earliest two years after publication. First attempt: Michael Rubinstein (Waterloo) posted a 164-page working draft, written by AI models at his request, that explains the argument for the Riemann zeta function (“The Alpöge–Claude proof of the Riemann hypothesis explained for the Riemann zeta function”, 21 Nov 2026; version 2, 265 pages, 22 Nov). By its own account it is work in progress and does not yet prove the key inequality (M); version 2 reduces (M), and with it RH, to thirteen numbered hypotheses. Alpöge has not yet published a human-readable account.

      Open tasks for readers

      1. Axiom report. Done by Sutherland: only propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Further independent replications: Omega Institute and rh-lean-check.
      2. Which literature is load-bearing. Walk the proof term of riemannHypothesis (e.g. with Lean.CollectAxioms-style traversal over constants) and list every GlobalTheory.Published.* statement it actually uses, with where it is discharged.
      3. Faithfulness of the literature statements. For each load-bearing statement, compare the Lean def with the published theorem it is named after. A weaker or tailored version is fine; a stronger-than-published one would need its own proof.
      4. Separate new mathematics from infrastructure. Mark which areas formalize known theory (étale cohomology, Weil bounds, resolution of singularities) and which carry the new argument (theta-lift chain, the VWin window bound).
      5. Glossary. One sentence per key definition on the spine (VWin, Row0b, TLiftK, recT …).

      Method and limits

      • Import graph parsed from the import lines of all 175,187 files; depth = longest import chain down to Mathlib.
      • Theorem and definition counts are line-start text matches (theorem, lemma, def, abbrev, structure, class, inductive): approximate.
      • “Direct proof found” means a line of the form theorem x : Name :=; proofs written differently are missed, so it is a lower bound.
